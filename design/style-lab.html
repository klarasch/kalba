Partybox: three directions

Three ways Partybox could look without emoji and without the default purple. Every phone is live: tap the buttons, burn the fuse, hold the impostor card. The picker switches all three at once so you compare the same moment. Turn the sound on first. Haptics only fire on Android Chrome outside this preview.

A · warm, light, Czech

Tácek

The pub table: beer mats, rubber stamps, tally marks on the bill.

Palette
cardboardfoambill redbottleink
Type
Alfa Slab One for stamped headlines and the words on cards, Rubik for UI. Both cover every Czech diacritic.
Icons
One-colour rubber-stamp line marks (hat, mask, bomb, head with card), slightly rotated, with a cheap SVG roughness filter so they look inked rather than drawn.
Signature detail
Score is kept as tally marks on a torn pub bill, the way a Czech waiter counts your beers. Cards are round beer mats sliding across the table.
Across the table
Dark ink on light card, above 12:1 contrast, slab type at 31px+. Best in daylight or a lit room; it glares a bit in a dark room, so it would need a "closing time" dark variant (dark wood, same mats).
B · loud, bright, poster

Riso

A risograph party flyer: two fluoro inks overprinted, halftone dots, chunky wide type.

Palette
paperfluoro pinkriso blueyellowink
Type
Unbounded (fat, wide, uppercase) for display, Figtree for body.
Icons
Each icon is two flat ink layers, pink and blue, printed 3px out of register with multiply blending, so the overlap turns purple like a real print.
Signature detail
Hard offset shadows, halftone dots and slightly misregistered layers. Buttons press down into their shadow.
Across the table
Text always stays ink black; colour is only for shapes, so contrast holds. It is the highest-energy option, but also the most tiring over a long evening.
C · dark, calm, night

Felt

A card table at night: green baize, ivory playing cards, brass chips.

Palette
baizeivorycard redinkbrass
Type
DM Serif Display for card indices and words, Instrument Sans for UI.
Icons
Each game gets its own suit: a filled silhouette pip (hat, mask, bomb, head) printed in card corners with a letter index: K, I, B, Č.
Signature detail
Every word is a playing card dealt face down and flipped; score is a stack of poker chips.
Across the table
Ivory cards on dark felt: low glare in a dim room, and the word is the brightest thing on screen. The safest option for evenings, but the least "Czech".

Klobouk

Every word is a card

  • Words are dealt in (mat slide, drop, or face-down flip).
  • "Mám to!" throws the card onto your team's pile, draws a tally mark or chip, plays a rising two-note pop and buzzes 20 ms.
  • Skip sends the card back the other way with a falling blip, so it reads as "not scored".
  • In the last 5 seconds the number pulses and ticks every second; at zero a buzzer and a "Čas!" card.
  • Later: a title card between rounds ("Kolo 2: jedno slovo") and an end-of-round count-up.

Bomba

A fuse you can watch

  • The fuse burns down along a curved path, with spark particles at the tip and an ash trail behind.
  • Burn time is random and hidden. Ticks speed up from 700 ms to about 110 ms, and the bomb starts to tremble near the end.
  • "Předat dál" makes a whoosh and a short buzz, so passing feels physical.
  • Explosion: flash, screen shake, debris in the direction's own shapes, a noise burst plus a low thump, and a long vibration.

Impostor

Privacy first, then drama

  • Hold to peek: a ring fills, the card flips, and it flips back the moment you let go, so a passing glance can't catch it.
  • The impostor and everyone else get the same haptic and the same sound. A different buzz would be heard around the table and give the impostor away.
  • Reveal: the room dims and a highlight hops between names like a roulette, slowing down over a drum roll, then the name slams in with a sting.

Na čele + everywhere

Small things, every screen

  • Na čele (not shown here, because the preview blocks motion sensors): full-screen 3·2·1, a green flash and chime for a correct tilt and an amber swoosh for a pass, both visible to the guessers across the table.
  • Home tiles are dealt in on load; buttons squash on press.
  • All sound is WebAudio synthesis, so no audio files are needed. One global sound toggle.
  • With prefers-reduced-motion: no shake, no particles, no flight. Cards swap in place and the explosion becomes a soft flash. Try the toggle above.

My pick

Go with Tácek, plus a dark "closing time" variant later.

It's the only one that feels like its own thing rather than a theme. The pub table is also where these games actually get played, and the details work as mechanics, not decoration: tally marks are how Czechs keep score, and mats get slid across the table just like the phone. Felt is the safe runner-up if most of your games happen late in dim rooms. Riso is the most fun for five minutes and the most tiring for three hours.
