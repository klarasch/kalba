Paper vs cards

Layout 2 is locked: dot grain, hard edges, tilted. Here only the relationship between the background and the cards changes, mostly by bringing each game's colour into its screen. Switch between screens to see Klobouk in pink, Impostor in green and Bomba in red. Dark mode is there too.

A: your pick

Neutral paper

Layout 2 as chosen. The game colour only shows in shadows and accents.

B: a hint

Dots in the game's ink

The paper grain gets printed in the game's colour. Subtle, but every game feels different.

C: a wash

Tinted paper

Each game's paper is washed in its colour. White cards pop more than on neutral paper.

D: all in

Full-colour screens

Each game is its colour, edge to edge. The home rows are filled with the same colours, so the home already tells you what's coming.

E: title band

Split screen

A colour band at the top holds the game info, and the card sits across the edge like it's being dealt onto the table.

F: coloured cards

Neutral paper, tinted cards

The opposite: the paper stays plain and the cards themselves carry the game's colour.

My pick

E, the split screen.

It colours every game clearly, so you know which one is on from across the room, but keeps your paper and white cards in the lower half where you actually read and tap. The card lying across the colour edge also looks like it's being dealt onto the table. D is the bolder runner-up: great energy, but a full pink screen for a 20-minute Klobouk round is a lot, and white text on pink is the weakest contrast here. B is the one to pick if you want the colour as a quiet hint only.
